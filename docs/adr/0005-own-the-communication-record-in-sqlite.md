# Own the communication record in SQLite

The Supervisor will keep the authoritative Transcript in SQLite, with separate state for Message Delivery, recipient Handling, and Conversation outcome plus correlated Codex turn identifiers. Delivery records distinguish `accepted`, `queued`, `injecting`, `injected`, `ambiguous`, `rejected`, `expired`, and `cancelled`; Handling records distinguish `starting`, `active`, `completed`, `failed`, and `interrupted`; Conversation records distinguish `open`, `completed`, `failed`, `expired`, `cancelled`, and `limit_reached`. Codex history is not a reliable communication ledger because some interactions are not persisted, while an in-memory or append-only text log would make correlation and state queries fragile; restart recovery remains outside the first prototype even though its evidence is durable.
